## Phase 2 — Team AI-On (Finding a Flat, Singapore)

**Scenario:** Group flat search. Shared context set and
edited quickly at session start — notably deliberate
context engagement, the fastest and most purposeful
context-setting observed across sessions.

**New joiner moment (AQ5):** Willson was designated to
step out and rejoin later with an additional constraint:
needing to travel to the west of Singapore daily, to
be worked around in the flat selection. On rejoining,
the agent incorporated Willson's location constraint
relatively quickly without requiring extensive
re-prompting — behaviour not strongly anchored to prior
context. Confirmation against logs recommended to
verify how quickly the constraint surfaced in AI
responses.

### Willson
- Research outputs providing too much information —
  wanted the result, not an auto-reflective prompt
  response that rehearses the question back.
- To-do / decision workflow found useful in team context.
- Summary function valued.
- Blocks of AI text disrupted conversational flow.

### Royston
- Tool good for generating ideas but did not feel like
  it moved the group into actual planning or
  decision-making.

### Group-level observations
- Each participant tended to pursue individual tangents
  with the AI. The AI paradoxically kept them loosely
  coordinated by making largely uncontroversial
  decisions that kept the group task-oriented — acting
  as a soft facilitator rather than a contributor.
- Depth-first individual AI interaction pattern rather
  than convergent group decision-making.
- General AI behaviour described as vague; factual
  accuracy uncertain across all three.

### Aly
- Observed that the AI was behaving like a teammate
  giving feedback and making decisions — in her view,
  unnecessary given that three people were already
  present to opinionate. The AI should be a tool, not
  a participant. This reinforces the loose coordination
  finding: the AI's uncontroversial, facilitative
  responses kept the group on task, but in doing so
  it stepped into a social role that at least one
  participant found inappropriate.

## Phase 3 — Team AI-Light (Trip Planning)

*Collated observations across all three participants.*

- All tasks completed under the AI-light condition.
  Reduced feature set did not impede task completion.

**Willson:** Preferred the AI-light version. Attributed
this to solving information overload — elements on the
right side of the interface felt overwhelming in the
full version.

**Royston:** AI-light felt faster and helped focus.
Seeing all the right-panel features simultaneously was
described as a lot to process.

**Aly (combined):** Too many functions in the full
version versus the neater AI-light interface. Did not
have to figure out the limits of each feature.
Explicitly preferred the simplicity. Separate note:
the component for replying to other human participants
should be made more visually prominent relative to AI
responses — in the current layout, AI activity visually
competes with human communication, inverting the
priority that a collaborative tool should have.

## Analytical Flags — Mapped to AQ Framework

**AQ1 — AI Routing Behaviour**
Clearer routing picture than Session 1. Willson engaged
with both modes and card features spontaneously — the
strongest unprompted feature engagement in this session.
Aly discovered mode distinction via the AI itself rather
than the UI — a self-directed onboarding strategy that
circumvents the interface legibility problem but is not
a scalable discovery path. Royston used Ask only and
avoided unknown features entirely. Research mode
producing templated non-retrieval outputs (Royston, Aly)
is a recurring signal: the mode label creates an
expectation of grounded retrieval that the prompt
structure does not currently support. Q4 post-survey
suggests all three reached adequate mode understanding
by session end with two reporting changed use — a
within-session learning signal and the clearest
positive AQ1 finding across sessions so far.

**AQ2 — Traceability and Trust**
Aly's post-survey Q1 (difficult) combined with her
observation that output felt like opinion rather than
fact is the primary AQ2 signal. Unlike Session 1 where
provenance concern was linked to absent internet search,
Aly's concern here is about the epistemic status of AI
output — she could not tell whether suggestions were
grounded in anything verifiable. Willson and Royston
rated source legibility positively, but this likely
reflects their narrower use of Ask mode, which has
clearer output framing, rather than genuine traceability
feature engagement. No formal traceability navigation
task run or captured in this session.

**AQ3 — Shared Understanding**
Context was set and edited quickly and deliberately at
the team phase start — the strongest proactive context
engagement in the study. This suggests context panel
discoverability improves substantially when participants
have a concrete, parameter-rich task (flat finding has
obvious shared criteria: location, budget, commute).
The group tangent-with-bot behaviour is the central
AQ3 finding for this session: participants operated in
parallel AI threads rather than building a shared frame.
The AI's facilitative, uncontroversial decision outputs
kept the group loosely coordinated, but this was an
emergent property of the AI's social behaviour rather
than the product of the shared context mechanism
functioning as designed. The context mechanism and the
AI's coordination function were effectively decoupled.

**AQ4 — Coordination Support**
The strongest session for AQ4 evidence across the study.
All three pre-exposure mental models were personal
productivity or note-taking frames — none anticipated
coordination support. The AI-light preference expressed
unanimously post-session reinforces the Session 1
finding: participants consistently prefer a less
featured, more focused interaction model. Aly's
observation about human reply prominence versus AI
response prominence is the most precise AQ4 finding
in this session: the tool's visual hierarchy currently
privileges AI output over human communication, which
inverts the intended relational priority in a
collaborative workspace. The depth-first AI tangent
pattern is the most nuanced AQ4 finding: the workspace
may subtly encourage individual AI interaction at the
expense of group convergence.

**AQ5 — Contextual Catch-Up**
Willson rejoined with a west-side commute location
constraint. The agent incorporated this constraint
relatively quickly without strongly anchoring to prior
session context — suggesting the context update
mechanism is responsive rather than rigid. This is a
positive AQ5 signal if confirmed by logs. The speed of
context uptake means that a new joiner who updates
the context panel can expect the AI to adapt promptly,
which is the core functional requirement for cold-start
catch-up to work. Log verification required to confirm
timing and the specific prompt/response pair where the
constraint first surfaced.
